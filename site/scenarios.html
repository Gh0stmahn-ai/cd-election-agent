How to read this

On today's inputs, the Senate is a coin flip at a generic ballot of R+1.2, and the House is a coin flip at a generic ballot of D+5.9. The generic ballot is currently D+8.9.

This is not a forecast of the generic ballot. It is the model answering a conditional: if the national environment were this instead of what it is, everything else being as it is today, here is what it would say. Ratings, state polls, the partisan index and the shape of the error are all held where they are; only the national number moves.

The grid runs from R+9 to D+16 in one-point steps and the page interpolates between them, which is why the numbers move smoothly rather than in jumps. Each point is 40,000 simulated elections rather than the 100,000 the published forecast uses: the comparison between points is what matters here, and using the same draws at every point means the difference between two points carries no simulation noise at all.
