    Where these numbers come from

    The forecast these results are graded against is the sealed final forecast. Made at 10:59 pm Eastern on November 2, before any polls opened, and sealed at 12:40 pm Eastern on November 3.

    Calls come from Wikipedia's 2026 Senate, House and governor articles, where editors mark a winner as the networks call each race. A call is taken here only after it has stood for four minutes and only once every poll in that state has closed, and a call that is taken down there is withdrawn here too. Nothing on this page projects a winner from a partial count.

    Counts come from the official results feeds of the seven states that publish one free and open to automated reading: Georgia, North Carolina, Texas, Iowa, Nebraska, Alaska and Florida. Other states' races show a call without a running count, and pick up vote shares once Wikipedia lists them. Alaska counts first choices on election night and runs its ranked-choice rounds on November 18.

    The grade. The Brier score is the average squared gap between the forecast's probability and what happened (0 is perfect; always saying 50% scores 0.25). It is set against the same score for the ratings alone, which is what a reader who just took Cook's rating would have got.
